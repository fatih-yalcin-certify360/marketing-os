# Social creative direction: evidence and implementation rules

Research checked: **14 September 2026**. Scope: campaign social images, their art direction and final branded composition. This is design guidance for implementation, not a claim that every rule is already implemented.

## What the sources support

| Evidence type | Finding | Consequence for this platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creative forecast | Adobe's 2026 forecast highlights sensory detail, human connection, playful surrealism and cultural specificity. It is a commercial trend forecast, not an advertising standard or proof of conversion lift for our audience. | Prefer a recognizable situation or meaningful visual metaphor over a generic office photograph. Select a style because it expresses the campaign idea. [Adobe forecast, December 2025](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2025/12/09/four-creative-trends-define-marketing-2026) |
| Creative examples | Adobe's January 2026 examples describe genuine interaction, emotional range and deliberately constructed, unexpected scenes. | Brief the action, relationships, materials and visual story. ## Platform specifications are separate from style

LinkedIn's current single-image specifications permit JPG, PNG and GIF up to **5 MB**. Recommended dimensions include **1200 × 628** landscape, **1200 × 1200** square and **720 × 900** portrait. Paid portrait images serve on mobile only. Introductory copy of 150 characters and headlines of 70 characters are truncation-avoidance recommendations, not their maximum input limits. These apply to LinkedIn paid single-image ads; do not silently apply them to every organic post or carousel. [LinkedIn specifications](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a426534/)

Meta's [Instagram Feed](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/image/instagram-feed) and [Facebook Feed](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/image/facebook-feed) specifications redirected to login during this check. Their current numeric limits were **not verified** here. Preserve placement-specific format configuration and check it in Ads Manager before delivery. Do not introduce an unverified “20% text” rejection rule, universal safe-zone percentage or “2026 standard” from third-party cheat sheets.

## Implementation decisions for our creative pipeline

The following are our editorial and engineering choices, informed by the sources above. They are not platform mandates.

1. **Start with a creative brief.** Capture the chosen persona's situation, campaign idea, verified course fact, funnel stage and intended next step. Record which persona details are assumptions. Every visible element should contribute to one understandable message.
2. **Choose a direction before a scene.** Candidate directions: a recognizable human moment; a conceptual object/metaphor; a conversational question; or a deliberately typographic composition. Use course-specific meaning. Avoid producing the same smiling person, laptop and gradient for every campaign.
3. **Plan image and copy together.** Store the focal subject, action, camera framing, background, negative-space region, copy position and optional bubble anchor. A bubble should belong to the scene and a plausible speaker; it must not cover the face, hands, important object or logo. A speech bubble is optional, not a new universal template.
4. **Keep exact branding deterministic.** Generate the scene without final lettering or logos. Compose the headline, question, bubble, CTA and official logo afterward using the current approved brand assets, exact color tokens and actual font files/weights. Brand colors apply exactly to graphic elements; natural skin, light and environmental colors need not be reduced to the brand palette. Never describe a substitute font as the brand font.
5. **Use genuine, concise copy.** Start with one focal question or statement; put detail in the caption or destination page. Our initial editorial limit is one headline and at most one secondary text unit. If the text does not fit, shorten it or choose another layout instead of shrinking it indefinitely. Fictional dialogue must not imply a real testimonial, accreditation, outcome or measured customer claim.
6. **Render for the placement.** Recompose portrait and landscape versions rather than cropping text off the same raster. Keep subject and copy in separate layout regions. Confirm text measurements, wrapping, clipping and contrast against the actual backing surface. If a brand-approved contrast pairing is unavailable, flag the issue instead of inventing colors and calling it compliant.
7. **Inspect the exported visual.** Check the actual asset at mobile feed size: legibility, hierarchy, awkward crops, anatomical artifacts, unwanted AI lettering, correct logo and font loading. Keep meaningful text available in the caption/accessible alternative; for our website use live text where the same presentation is possible. [W3C images-of-text guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text.html)
8. **Make variation and evaluation explicit.** Store concept, scene prompt, brand version, font references, overlay copy and output dimensions. Label a different concept separately from an A/B copy variant. Start with a small shortlist and test against the campaign's chosen outcome; visual polish, a trend label or an AI review score does not establish effectiveness.

## Minimum review record

For each delivered creative, retain: the persona/campaign version; the creative rationale; exact overlay text; original scene asset; rendered export; brand/font version; placement profile; deterministic validation results; and any unresolved review notes. This makes “why this image?” answerable without claiming that a generated image proves audience response.

## Implemented slice — 14 September 2026

New organic LinkedIn, Instagram and Facebook image proposals include a structured `creativeBrief` in the existing content-generation call. There is no additional art-director model call per image. The brief records the audience insight, mechanism, rationale, concrete scene, composition, text treatment/position, brand integration and avoid list. Previous scene descriptions travel with earlier campaign copy to discourage repeating the same idea. Missing image briefs and overly long overlay copy trigger the existing single repair round; a full revision cannot silently replace a creative image with a legacy layout.

The image prompt combines that per-piece brief with the selected concept, funnel message and current brand profile. It reserves the same text rectangle that the renderer uses and a bottom brand-signature area. These bounds are **our composition choices**, not platform safe-zone specifications. Images are requested for the full output format; legacy backgrounds retain their earlier generation behavior. The generator supplies the scene without lettering. The compositor supports a speech bubble, an editorial panel and an image-led caption treatment. Its A/B variants keep the same image framing and text position, varying graphic treatment/colors; these are design variations, not independent concepts or evidence of effectiveness.

When the image adapter requests a different native aspect ratio, `creativeSourceZones` maps the visible crop and both reserved regions back to source pixels. The prompt receives that map so its subject placement accounts for the renderer's center crop. This does not detect subjects in the returned bitmap. The renderer records `fontSource` so the card distinguishes loaded brand files from system fallback.

Text is measured against the actual loaded fonts, fitted within bounded regions and never silently shortened in creative layouts. Palette-only contrast selection requires 4.5:1. When the supplied palette offers no readable pair, rendering reports an error instead of inventing a new brand color. Portal-linked labels load the release's pinned font/logo files; missing files fail before image generation. Manual/demo labels without licensed font files still use system fallback, so their font names alone do not establish authentic typography.

`RenderSpec` saves the creative brief with the exact overlay copy and brand colors/fonts. The content card exposes it under **Creatieve beeldbrief**, with a copy action. Manual copy edits and copy-only AI revisions preserve the image direction, headline and original scene; image-only revisions preserve the existing text/direction while refining the scene. Changing the entire concept requires a new content generation or a combined revision. Existing images are not retroactively modified; a combined revision can supply a new creative brief to an older asset.

Checks cover schema compatibility, per-piece prompt inputs, persisted A/B metadata, one repair, revision boundaries, contrast, wrapping and SVG escaping. Exported PNGs were inspected with clearly artificial local test artwork, not paid AI images. This validates composition and text handling. It does **not** prove that a live model follows the subject placement perfectly or that the audience will respond; the final generated image still requires visual review and campaign measurement.
